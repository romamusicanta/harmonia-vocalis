## Our philosophy

Listening to art music is not the preserve of privileged circles, and offering it in a variety of social settings is the mission that **Maestro Claudio Maria Micheli**, who has led the choir since its founding and has been the architect of its growth, shares with us and reminds us never to betray. In him the singers of **Harmonia Vocalis** have found a Conductor able to give voice to this philosophy.

## What we have sung since 2019

At the end of 2019 came the first three concerts: Haydn’s *Missa in Angustiis* in October in Rome and in November in Segni, and the New Year’s Concert (opera excerpts) in Velletri.

From 2020 the outbreak of COVID-19 made singing in concerts impossible: only in December 2021 was a concert held, in Cerveteri, following the rules that the Conference of the Regions then asked choirs to observe (masks, distancing…).

The following years saw the Choir grow, in numbers and in singing ability, and perform in many concerts in Rome and the surrounding province.

Our collaboration with the **Coro Ruggero Giovannelli** has created a choral group of more than 70 voices, able to perform almost the entire sacred and secular repertoire, from the Baroque to the twentieth century and on to the more recent music of Ennio Morricone, with whom the Conductor collaborated in the early 2000s.

The two choirs have shared a similar repertoire for years, which gives the Conductor a sizeable ensemble for collaborating with organisations specialising in the production of musical and cultural events.

Since Harmonia Vocalis was founded we have taken on many works: Mozart’s Requiem and Great Mass, Rossini’s Stabat Mater and Petite Messe Solennelle, Puccini’s Messa di Gloria, Orff’s Carmina Burana, Handel’s Messiah, Fauré’s Requiem, Vivaldi’s Gloria, Liszt’s Missa Choralis, vocal compositions and opera excerpts.

More great works, now in preparation, await us on stage.

## What drives us

We sing to deepen the awareness of those who listen of the value of the musical message, whether sacred or secular: a universal language that crosses barriers and fosters human relationships. For this reason we also support charitable initiatives through music.

When the audience applauds at the end of a concert we are happy; even more so when it is the orchestra that shows us its appreciation.

> The Maestro reminds us that we are amateur singers, but we must aspire to sing like professionals.
